How it decides

  • Released to tech only when the note shows written approval: signed or e-signed estimate, signed authorization on file, or approved by text or email with the reply saved.
  • Held: verbal only when the customer said yes, OK'd or approved it on the phone, by voicemail or in person, and nothing was signed. Same if someone else (a spouse, say) approved by phone.
  • Held: no approval recorded when the estimate went out but there's no yes, or the plan is to sign at pickup.
  • Held: approval doesn't cover the full estimate when part is signed and part isn't, or the approved amount is less than the estimate.
  • Held: check the note when the note is unclear, e.g. "approved" with no word on how, or a text approval with no saved reply.
  • Do not start: declined when the customer said no.

Two reads per note. First, fixed rules that catch wording like "did not sign", "will sign at pickup" and "signed for the brakes but verbal on the rotors". Second, TypeSafe reads the note on its own. An RO is released only when both see written approval. If they disagree, or TypeSafe isn't sure, it's held and both answers are shown. If TypeSafe can't be reached, the rules decide alone and still hold anything that isn't plainly written.

What it doesn't do
  • It doesn't listen to or record phone calls. It only reads the notes you paste or type.
  • It doesn't connect to or change your shop system. Nothing is written back to the RO.
  • It doesn't send texts. The text drafts are for your writers to copy and send themselves.
  • It's a check before the tech starts, not a legal guarantee. Written approval gives you something to show at pickup; it can't promise a bill won't be disputed.
